Eating: conscious process by which an individual ingests food from the environment.

Nutrition: mostly involuntary process by which nutrients that were obtained from food are absorbed and used by our cells.

FOODS AND NUTRIENTS

NUTRIENTS

BIOMOLECULES

three functions:

- Energetic function (provide calories to perform functions)
- Structural function (used to build and repair our organism)
- Regulatory function (essential for the correct functioning of the organism)

The food wheel

Foods are categorised according to the main nutrients they provide:

The food wheel is a visual tool that classifies foods into six groups. 

The size of each segment is proportional to the recommended daily consumption of that group of food. The foods found near the centre in groups 1, 2, 3 and 4 (sweets, cakes, butter, bacon, cold meats and cheese) should be eaten in moderation.

The food wheel also includes the need to drink water and to exercise.

Dietary fibre

Fibre cannot be digested directly, but intestinal bacteria can, so our bodies can indirectly obtain some nutrients from it.

It also softens the faeces, making it easier to go to the toilet and keeping the digestive tract healthier.

ENERGY IN FOODS

Chemical energy obtained from food is measured in calories.

1 calorie is the amount of heat (= energy) needed to raise the temperature of 1 gram of water by 1 degree

Talking about food and nutrition, it is more common to use kilocalories instead of calories (1 kcal = 1.000 cal). For example, a medium sized apple has approximately 75 kcal or 75.000 calories.

ENERGY IN FOODS

1 gram of carbohydrates = 4 kcal

1 gram of lipids = 9 kcal

1 gram of protein = 4 kcal

ENERGY IN FOODS

Basal metabolic rate (BMR)

Amount of energy a person needs per day just to keep its body functioning at rest. 

The BMR depends on body weight, height, age and biological sex.

The BMR DOES NOT depend on the amount of exercise the person does.

ENERGY IN FOODS

Daily calorie need (DCN)

Amount of energy a person needs per day to maintain its weight. 

The DCN of a person is equal to their BMR + the kilocalories they use to do exercise.

![Image](UNIT%203%20-%20EATING%20AND%20NUTRITION%20%28students%29_artifacts/image_000018_e0ff9f5d4a3c873a57900bd9d5b56eecd7464a63d27ffc6cea8e25acb89414a9.png)

If the kcal intake of a person is higher than the DCN, the person will gain body weight (glycogen/fat/muscle)

DCN

If the kcal intake of a person is lower than the DCN, the person will lose body weight (glycogen/fat/muscle)

HEALTHY DIETS

A healthy diet:

- Provides as many kilocaries as the person’s  DCN
- Has a right balance of macronutrients: 
    - 60 % of kcal from carbohydrates
    - 25 % of kcal from fats
    - 15 % of kcal from proteins.
- Include all the necessary nutrients, including micronutrients: fibre, vitamins and mineral salts.

              Book activities: 7 and 14.

NUTRITIONAL DISEASES

Nutritional diseases are caused by an inadequate diet:

- Undernutrition: insufficient intake of nutrients
- Overnutrition: Excessive intake of nutrients
- Defficiencies:  Lack of specific nutrients in the diet

NUTRITIONAL DISEASES

Undernutrition

Condition that occurs after a prolonged period of shortage of calories and/or nutrients. It causes weight loss, decreased mental capacity, skin problems, hair thinning and hair loss.

It can be caused by the inaccessibility of food due to socio-economic conditions or by psychological disorders like anorexia nervosa or bulimia.

NUTRITIONAL DISEASES

Undernutrition

Anorexia nervosa and bulimia

- Both are psychological disorders in which the perception of the own body is distorted.
- 
- People that suffer from anorexia nervosa self-restrict the amount of food they ingest to extreme limits.
- 
- People that suffer from bulimia eat excessively and then self-induce vomiting.
- 
- Both cause severe undernutrition, leading to depression, anxiety, cardiovascular diseases, kidney problems and in extreme cases even death.

NUTRITIONAL DISEASES

Deficiency diseases

Condition caused by a prolonged shortage of a specific type of nutrient. 

They are usually caused by a lack of minerals salts or vitamins.

	Mineral salts deficiencies:

- Lack of iron, can cause anemia.
- Lack of calcium, can cause osteoporosis.

Vitamin deficiencies:

- Lack of vitamin A causes xerophthalmia.
- Lack of vitamin B3 causes pellagra.
- Lack of vitamin C causes scurvy.
- Lack of vitamin D causes rickets.

Prevention of nutritional diseases

- Acquire healthy eating habits from childhood.

- Reduce the consumption of fast food and sugary drinks.

- Exercise and have an active lifestyle.
- 
- Do not make critical comments about physical appearance or weight.

- Detect nutrition-related problems early to take steps to stop them.

**THE PRIMARY SECTOR**

1. **AGRICULTURE**

**1.1 Factors**

- **Natural factors these factors explain why crops are limited to some regions or why it is difficult for them to develop in other areas.**

1. **Climate: which includes humidity, rainfall, sunshine…. affects how plants grow.**
2. **The density and fertility of the soil.**
3. **Relief**

**- Human factors**

1. **Mechanisation of farm work.**
2. **Modern sprinkler and drip irrigation systems** make it possible to reduce water consumption.
3. **Chemicals** are used to make the soil more fertile.
4. **Biological techniques** make it possible to fight parasites that affect crops.

**Currently, agriculture is divided into two different types:**

1. **Traditional agriculture**

The economies of **developing countries** are dominated by traditional agriculture, **with limited mechanisation and low productivity.** It is largely limited to **subsistence farming and self-consumption** by farmers and their families.

2. **Mechanised agriculture**

**In contrast, agriculture in countries with developed economies is highly mechanised and productivity is high. The objective is large-scale commercial production.**

- 1.2. **Types of crop farming**

1. **Dry and irrigation farming: In dry farming, crops are watered exclusively by precipitation. In irrigation farming, water is brought from rivers and reservoirs using a costly system of channels. This makes it possible to obtain higher yields than dry farming.**
2. **Intensive and extensive farming: In extensive farming, land is left fallow. It is left uncultivated for a year to allow it to regenerate. Crop yields depend on investment in mechanization, fertilisers,etc. In intensive farming, the land produces very high yields: it is made to produce several crops a year by using irrigation, greenhouses and fertilisers.**
3. **Polyculture and monoculture: In polyculture, agricultural areas are divided into several plots of land, where different species are cultivated. By contrast, monoculture is highly specialised, with a single product being planted over a large area. This method is commonly used on tropical plantations of coffee, bananas, tea, etc., or in the extensive dry farming of crops, such as grapes and cereals.**

2. **LIVESTOCK FARMING**

**2.1 Factors Influencing Livestock Farming**

**Physical Factors**

1. **Cold and Mountain Areas:** Raising animals is common in cold places like mountains or high plateaus. The land is not good for crops, but there is pasture for animals.
2. **Semi-Desert and Desert Areas:** In dry areas, farmers move around to find water and grass for their animals.

**Human Factors**

1. **Population Density:** In areas with fewer people (like the Pampas in Argentina), livestock farming is more common because it needs less labour than growing crops.
2. **Modern Changes (Green Revolution):**
    - Animals can now be fed with crops instead of just pasture.
    - Veterinary care and farming technology have improved.
    - Livestock can be raised in more places, not just where there is natural grass.

**2.2 Types of Livestock Farming**

**Extensive Livestock Farming**

Animals graze freely in fields or meadows. There are three main types:

1. **Nomadic:**
    - Farmers move with animals to find food and water.
    - Common in developing countries.
    - Examples: Tuareg in the Sahara, Sámi in Lapland.
2. **Transhumant:**
    - Animals move seasonally (e.g., to cool places in summer).
    - Less common today in developed countries.
3. **Sedentary:**
    - Animals stay in one place to graze.
    - Most common in developed countries.

**Animals Used:** Sheep, cattle, goats, horses.  
**Productivity:** Higher in developed countries due to better care and investment. In poorer countries, it's often just for the farmer’s own use (subsistence farming).

**Intensive Livestock Farming**

- Animals are kept on farms in buildings (not in open fields).
- They are given feed, and the living conditions are monitored by vets.
- Needs more workers and money, but gives high results (more meat, milk, etc.).
- Common in developed countries.

**Animals Used:** Cattle, pigs, poultry (chickens), rabbits.

3. **FORESTRY**

**3.1  Factors**

**Forestry** is the practice of exploiting wood from forests. The type of forest in a region depends on three main factors:

1. **Climate** (temperature and rainfall)
2. **Soil quality** (fertility and texture)
3. **Relief** (the shape and slope of the land)

Traditionally, wood was mainly used as **fuel and as a building material**. However, in developed countries, the use of wood as fuel has nearly disappeared because of **new technologies and energy sources**. Likewise, building materials like **cement, metal, and glass have replaced wood** in construction. Besides wood, forests provide other products such as **cork** and **resin**.

The **profitability** of using wood depends on how fast trees grow. Since trees take many years to mature, wood production is more profitable **where trees grow faster**. Therefore, the wood used for the **paper** and **furniture** industries has expanded in **tropical and boreal regions**,That is why forestry has expanded in:

- **Tropical regions** (large forests, fast-growing species)
- **Boreal regions** (cold but vast forests)
- In **temperate zones** like Spain, tree plantations have become important.

Industrial-scale exploitation of forests and rainforests causes **deforestation** and **soil erosion**. These effects happen locally but can also affect the global climate and biodiversity over time.

Public awareness today encourages **forest preservation** and **regeneration**. Regeneration is the process of re-establishing forests, either naturally or by planting.

**3.2 Types of forestry**

Traditional forestry involved three main methods:

1. **Tree-felling**: Cutting down all the trees in an area. The land may be replanted or left alone to regenerate naturally. Sometimes, the cleared land is used for farming or grazing.
2. **Thinning**: Cutting down only some trees in a forest to give the remaining ones more space and resources to grow better.
3. **Pruning**: Periodically cutting branches from trees without cutting down the whole tree. This helps trees continue growing wood while staying healthy.

An example of traditional forestry is the **dehesa** in Spain, where some trees were removed (thinned) to create pasture for livestock, while the remaining trees were pruned for wood.

Since the 20th century, large plantations of fast-growing species like **eucalyptus**, **Canadian poplar**, and **plane tree** have been created. These plantations use land that:

- Was once agricultural but is no longer farmed,
- Has been cleared of forests,
- Or is degraded land in developing countries.

These plantations help meet the demand for wood from industries.

4. **FISHING**

4.1. Factors Influencing Fishing:

- Fishing mostly takes place in ocean waters.
- Best fishing grounds are in shallow waters.
- Areas where warm and cold ocean currents meet have large concentrations of fish (shoals).
- Freshwater fishing exists but is less common compared to ocean fishing.

4.2. Types of Fishing:

1. Traditional Fishing:
    - Common in developing countries, mainly for self-supply or local markets.
    - Uses artisanal practices and simple equipment such as:
        - Small boats
        - Nets
        - Harpoons are thrown at the prey, and the tip stay hooked in the body.
        - Hooks, a hook is a type of tackle used in pole fishing.
        - Fish pots
    - Happens in freshwater bodies, along shorelines, or coastal waters.
    - Shellfish gathering (mussels, cockles, crabs) is a common activity and can be commercial in developed countries.
2. Commercial Fishing:
    - Mainly in developed countries for commercial profit.
    - Includes two main types:
        - Coastal Fishing:
            - Fishing near the coast or in large freshwater bodies.
            - Small boats are used for 1-2 day trips.
            - Nets and paternoster lines (long lines with multiple hooks) are common tools.
            - Requires minimal technology.
        - Deep-Sea Fishing:
            - Done exclusively in the open ocean.
            - Uses large fleets equipped with sonar and radar to detect fish shoals.
            - Ships are large, spend months at sea, and include factory ships for processing and freezing fish onboard.

3. Fishing Techniques:

- Trawling:
    - Uses large funnel-shaped nets dragged behind trawler boats.
    - Efficient for catching large quantities of fish.
- Electrofishing with Suction:
    - A device generates electrical discharges in the water to direct fish towards the fishing boats.
    - Boats have large funnels to suck fish up.

4. Aquaculture:

- Involves farming fish, crustaceans, molluscs, and algae in controlled environments.
- Common aquaculture practices include:
    - Platforms for farming mussels.
    - Ocean and river fish farms.
- Species raised include:
    - Trout
    - Gilthead
    - Sea bass
    - Turbot
    - Various algae types
